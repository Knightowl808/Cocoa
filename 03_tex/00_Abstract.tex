\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

This thesis investigates whether Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) quantile regression produces better out-of-sample distributional forecasts of cocoa futures volatility than established benchmarks, and whether augmenting the model with the El~Ni\~no--Southern Oscillation (ENSO) climate index improves long-horizon upper-tail forecasts. Daily Open-High-Low-Close data for ICE London cocoa front-month futures spanning January~1985 to December~2025 (10,376 trading days) are used to construct a Yang-Zhang volatility proxy, validated against realized volatility computed from approximately one year of five-minute intraday data. Forecasts at six horizons (1~day to 12~months) are evaluated by quantile loss and QLIKE against five competing models, with Model Confidence Sets used to assess statistical significance.

For the first research question, HAR-based quantile regression dominates both the rolling historical volatility benchmark and GARCH(1,1) at every horizon and under every loss criterion examined. Historical volatility and GARCH are excluded from the 90\% Model Confidence Set at every horizon under the upper-tail ($\tau = 0.95$) quantile loss criterion; at twelve months, the best benchmark is more than 67\% above HAR-QR in quantile loss. Direct quantile estimation provides a statistically significant advantage over the HAR-OLS pseudo-quantile at short horizons, while the two are statistically indistinguishable at medium and long horizons. Upper-quantile coverage is near the nominal $5\%$ at one day and one week but degrades progressively as the horizon extends.

For the second research question, the Ni\~no~3.4 index reduces upper-tail quantile loss by $30.9\%$ at the twelve-month horizon and lifts HAR-X-QR to the top Model Confidence Set rank from three months onward. Three robustness diagnostics indicate, however, that the cumulative gain is concentrated entirely in the 2022--2024 cocoa supply-crisis window, follows a lag structure that contradicts the agronomic transmission hypothesis, and does not replicate on Arabica coffee or raw sugar. The ENSO gain is therefore interpreted as a regime-conditional, sample-specific effect rather than a replicable structural climate channel.

The thesis makes three contributions: the first application of HAR-QR to an agricultural commodity, the first evaluation at horizons extending to twelve months, and the first empirical test of an ENSO-augmented HAR-QR specification for cocoa.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cocoa futures, volatility forecasting, HAR model, quantile regression, ENSO, long-horizon forecasting, Yang-Zhang estimator
